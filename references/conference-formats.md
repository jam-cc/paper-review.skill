# Review format guidance

Use this reference when selecting the review structure. These are writing templates, not authoritative venue policies. Forms and scoring scales change by venue, track, and year. Use the user's actual form first; otherwise consult the current official reviewer instructions when available. Do not infer a numeric scale from a conference name alone.

## Default review

When no form is supplied, use:

```text
Review of [paper title or submission ID]

1. Summary
2. Strengths
3. Weaknesses
4. Questions and Suggestions
5. References
```

Keep paragraphs focused on evidence and implications. Number individual concerns when this helps cross-referencing. Separate Questions and Suggestions if the requested form has separate fields. Omit the References section when there are no external citations unless the form requires it.

## Conference forms

For venues such as NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, CVPR, ECCV, AAAI, ACM MM, and ECML PKDD, map the analysis onto the actual field names rather than assuming these venues share a rubric. A form might ask for soundness, presentation, contribution, reproducibility, ethics, recommendation, or confidence, but the names and allowed values must come from that form.

If scores are requested and the rubric is available, use its labels and values exactly. Explain each score with the evidence supporting the assessment, including strengths where relevant. If the rubric is unavailable, deliver the prose and state that the numeric fields still need the current rubric. Do not fabricate official score bands.

## Journals

If the journal requests major and minor concerns, separate issues that affect validity or contribution from issues addressable through explanation or presentation. Section-by-section commentary may help for a long manuscript, but avoid repeating the same concern in several places.

Use recommendation labels from the supplied journal form. Do not assume that a journal uses conference-style scores or a numeric confidence field.

## Workshops

Judge the submission against the workshop's stated scope and criteria, including preliminary work where invited. Adjust the depth and length to the form. Do not preselect an acceptance recommendation merely because the submission is for a workshop.

## Across formats

- Follow the requested length or field limits. Otherwise use enough detail to establish the main arguments without padding.
- Review the work, not guessed author identities or affiliations.
- Explain uncertainty caused by unreadable material, missing supplements, limited source access, or unfamiliarity with a method.
- Keep author-facing comments separate from confidential committee comments when the form requires it.
- The user's formatting requirements override the skill's default prose and citation conventions.
